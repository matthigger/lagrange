Lagrange multipliers, visually

Walk along a constraint: at its best points, the gradient of f lines up with the gradient of the constraint.

Why ∇f = λ∇g

Problem: find the max or min of f(x) while staying on the path g(x) = 0. Here f(x) = x0x1 and the path is the unit circle, g(x) = x02 + x12 − 1 = 0.

  1. Walk along ∇f and f goes up; walk against it and f goes down. Walk perpendicular to it and f doesn't change.
  2. ∇g is always perpendicular to the path. If it weren't, walking along the path would change g, and g(x) wouldn't stay 0.
  3. Where ∇f and ∇g line up (same or opposite direction), we're at a constrained max or min: ∇f is perpendicular to the path too (like ∇g, step 2), so walking along the path doesn't change f (step 1).
∇f(x) = λ ∇g(x) g(x) = 0

Try this

  • Drag the point around the circle. Where does the red arrow (the part of ∇f along the path) vanish? Find all four places.
    AnswerAt (±1/√2, ±1/√2), where ∇f lines up with ∇g. Same signs: maxima (f = 1/2). Opposite signs: minima (f = −1/2).
  • Press Climb from a few starting points. Where does it stop, and why there?
    AnswerClimb steps along the red arrow, so it stops where the red arrow vanishes: where ∇f lines up with ∇g (step 3).
  • Switch to 3D and follow the path lifted onto the surface.
    AnswerIts high and low points are the answers. The surface itself is a saddle, with no max or min at all.
